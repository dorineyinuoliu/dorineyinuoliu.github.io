Who goes cold in New York?

Heat complaints in the 2025–26 winter: when people complained, how much the weather explains, where the complaints cluster, and which buildings show up again and again.

The question

From October 1 to May 31, New York landlords have to keep apartments at 68°F during the day when it's below 55°F outside, and at least 62°F at night. Tenants who don't get heat can report it to 311, so the 311 data is a fairly direct record of who was cold, and when.

I asked four questions, one for each chart: When during the winter did people complain? How much does the weather explain it? Where are the complaints, and does that line up with income? Is it the same buildings over and over?

Data and methods

  • Pulled only HEAT/HOT WATER complaints from October 1, 2025 to May 31, 2026 through the NYC Open Data API instead of downloading the whole dataset: 341,824 complaints.
  • Split them by the “additional details” field. 303,434 were no-heat complaints; the rest were about hot water only.
  • Matched every day of the season to the overnight low at NOAA's Central Park station. All 243 days were there.
  • For the map, divided no-heat complaints by renter households in each ZIP code area, because heat complaints come almost entirely from renters. Areas with fewer than 500 renter households are gray.
  • Kept repeat complaints from the same address on the same day in the citywide counts, and used “days with at least one complaint” for the building chart, where repeats matter most.
  • Re-ran the main counts in SQL (DuckDB) to check the pandas numbers.

Limits and next steps

A complaint is a tenant's report, not a confirmed violation, so the next step would be adding the city's housing violation data. Public housing tenants mostly report problems to NYCHA instead of 311, which means areas with a lot of public housing probably look better on the map than they really are. I'd also like to repeat the building chart over several winters.
